_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

Today the criminals took the service entrance again: a French bank lost $133 million through a service provider, an MFA bypass is being exploited against SonicWall VPNs, and AI agents are busy both factoring RSA keys and looting emergency dispatch records. Add a courtroom twist over a bugged meeting room at OpenAI, and the theme of the day is trust - in vendors, in old crypto, and in who can walk into your conference room. Pour something strong.

### Hackers Steal $133 Million From French Bank Through Service Provider

Attackers broke into Crédit Mutuel Arkéa via one of its service providers and pushed through roughly $133 million in fraudulent transactions before the bank started suspending systems to stop the bleeding.
Your vendors' access is your access - one outsourced connection turned a cooperative bank into a very expensive lesson in supply-chain trust.

**What to do:** Inventory your critical service providers, scope their access to the minimum, and require out-of-band verification for every high-value transaction.

---

### SonicWall SonicOS MFA Bypass Exploited In The Wild

A high-severity SonicOS flaw, CVE-2026-19868, lets an attacker holding credentials for one login method sail past SAML checks on SSL-VPN logins - including MFA enforced through SAML.
The patch is out, CISA added it to the KEV catalog amid signs of real-world exploitation, and anything internet-facing and unpatched is now officially racing the exploit bots.

**What to do:** Patch your SonicWall appliances today and review VPN logs for authentications that should never have succeeded.

---

### DeepMind AI Agents Cut The Cost Of Breaking RSA-1024

Google DeepMind's AI agents, working alongside cryptographers, found cheaper ways to forge RSA-1024 signatures and to factor the keys behind them.
Nothing is broken today and RSA-2048 is still fine - but the cost curve for attacking 1024-bit keys just bent downward, and old keys never die on schedule.

**What to do:** Retire every RSA-1024 key and certificate you still run, and keep your TLS and code-signing inventory at 2048-bit or stronger.

---

### AI Agents Help Lone Hacker Ransack Taipei Emergency Records

A single attacker in Taiwan used off-the-shelf BOSSAI AI agents to pull emergency dispatch records covering seven Taipei districts, and the same fleet uncovered a fire-department platform leaking patient data back to 2018 - ECG readings and ambulance video included.
One human, an army of AI agents doing the recon: vulnerability discovery now scales without payroll, and exposed data gets found fast.

**What to do:** Assume automated agents are mapping your internet footprint around the clock - find your exposed data before their AI does.

---

### Former OpenAI Engineer Pleads Not Guilty In Bugging Case

The ex-OpenAI engineer accused of planting a covert audio device in an executive meeting room pleaded not guilty in federal court, setting the stage for a trial that will make every "can we talk in here?" conversation awkward.
No exploit required - just a badge, a quiet afternoon, and a meeting room nobody thought to sweep for microphones.

**What to do:** Give physical security some love: audit access to sensitive rooms, sweep for listening devices, and set rules for what can be plugged in where.
