_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

Today's lineup is a masterclass in weaponized trust: a password manager whose GitHub account got hijacked, an infostealer that plays corporate IT admin with your browser's policy engine, and zero-days that hit Mitel's MiCollab before the vendor could even publish an advisory. On top of that, malware is now posing as take-home job assessments, because applying for work wasn't stressful enough. Bring coffee.

### 1Password Breached via GitHub, Vaults Somehow Still Fine

An attacker hijacked an employee's GitHub account, pushed malicious code into 1Password's backend systems, and walked off with customer records and internal source code - the actual password vaults stayed locked.
Even a vault-sparing breach is ammunition: stolen customer data plus source code makes the next wave of fake "1Password security alerts" terrifyingly convincing.

**What to do:** Treat any urgent 1Password-themed email as hostile until proven otherwise, and make sure 2FA is enabled on your account.

---

### SYS010 Enrolls Your Browser into Fake Corporate Management

A new SYS01 infostealer variant, spread through Meta ads, abuses browser enterprise management features to force-install a malicious extension that hoovers up Gmail and Outlook messages.
Your browser starts obeying "corporate policy" written by an attacker - and almost nobody has ever looked at chrome://policy in their life.

**What to do:** Check chrome://policy and edge://policy for management entries you didn't create, and never install extensions pushed by ads.

---

### Mitel MiCollab Zero-Days Exploited Before the Vendor Could Warn

Attackers exploited previously unknown flaws in Mitel's MiCollab unified comms platform before Mitel even published its advisory, in what looks like an extortion campaign, with researchers saying AI tooling likely helped.
CISA has since added five MiCollab flaws to its Known Exploited catalog - the window between "zero-day" and "your unpatched PBX is someone's entry point" keeps shrinking.

**What to do:** Patch MiCollab now and pull internet-facing UC/telephony gear behind a VPN or the management network.

---

### Drupal's Installer Fix Leaves a Hole Attackers Are Already Using

Drupal shipped a "highly critical" patch for its installer (CVE-2026-136716), then admitted the earlier fix left a gap - and attackers are already exploiting it to take over sites.
The flaw is an improper error-condition check tied to the installer: if your site went through the vulnerable window, "patched" does not automatically mean "safe."

**What to do:** Update Drupal core to the latest release immediately, then verify the installer is gone and your site's configuration is intact.

---

### Take-Home Job Assessments Now Deliver Malware to Developers

Thirteen malicious NuGet packages posing as WinUI 3 and WPF button controls are circulating as fake take-home coding assessments in job interview processes - a recruiter's email that pwns your dev box.
The trust model is brutal: applicants run the code themselves, on their own machines, because the "employer" asked nicely.

**What to do:** Run any take-home assessment in a throwaway VM, and vet NuGet packages (publisher, download counts, source) before your restore step trusts them.
