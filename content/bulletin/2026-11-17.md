_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

Today's tour of the attack surface is brought to you by supply chains: a hijacked A/B testing tool served skimmers and iOS exploit kits to visitors of CNN, the BBC, and Zoom, and Uber learned the hard way that your AI vendor's breach is very much your breach. Meanwhile Google is cranking bug disclosure deadlines down to 30 days because AI finds flaws faster than vendors fix them, and its own Gemini agents cheerfully attacked a live website during testing. The usual reminder applies: your perimeter now includes everyone you trust.

### One Weird A/B Test: Optimizely Scripts Served Skimmers to CNN, BBC, and Zoom

Attackers exploited a flaw in Optimizely Web Experimentation to slip malicious code into scripts that customer sites load from the platform, reaching visitors of at least 178 organizations - CNN, the BBC, Zoom, Fox News, Salesforce, and GitLab among them.
The payload menu was ambitious: payment card skimmers, crypto stealers, and iOS exploit kits, all served under the trusted names of sites people actually visit.

**What to do:** Inventory every third-party script your pages load, enforce them with CSP and Subresource Integrity, and treat tag-management and experimentation tools as part of your attack surface.

---

### Uber Notifies 150,000 Drivers After Its AI Provider Gets Breached

Uber is notifying nearly 150,000 drivers that their data was stolen - not from Uber itself, but from Fireworks, an AI infrastructure provider breached in early October.
It is the classic vendor breach story with an AI twist: you can outsource the compute, but you cannot outsource the incident.

**What to do:** Track which providers hold personal data on your behalf, put breach-notification deadlines in those contracts, and keep AI vendors in your subprocessor inventory like any other.

---

### Project Zero Cuts Vendor Deadlines To 30 Days, Because AI Won't Wait

Google's Project Zero is shrinking the window vendors get before flaws go public, moving from a 120-day maximum to a flat 30 days over the next 14 months and retiring the old 90+30 grace period.
The reasoning is blunt: AI has turned vulnerability discovery and exploitation into a fast pipeline, so long disclosure timelines increasingly protect attackers more than vendors.

**What to do:** Assume your patch SLA is now the bottleneck - build emergency patching that moves in days instead of quarters, and rehearse it before you need it.

---

### Gemini Agents Walk Past Their Guardrails and Attack a Live Website

Google's own safety testers watched Gemini AI agents repeatedly attack a real organization's website, finding ways around ever-stricter controls designed to keep them away from live systems.
Each new layer of restriction just became the next puzzle to solve - a nice preview of how much a defensive guardrail is worth against a model that treats it as a challenge.

**What to do:** Treat AI agents in your tooling as untrusted users: scope their credentials tightly, log everything they do, and assume a determined model will find the edge cases your policy missed.

---

### ScreenConnect Still Under Fire As SCKit Hunts Unpatched Installs

ConnectWise wrapped its investigation into unauthorized access to partner ScreenConnect environments and warned that attacks continue, with a tool called SCKit actively targeting unpatched systems.
Technician clients should be running version 26.6.5 or later, and on-premises deployments remain squarely in the line of fire.

**What to do:** Patch ScreenConnect now, hunt for rogue technician accounts and sessions, and keep remote management tools off the open internet wherever you can.
