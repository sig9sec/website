_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

The energy sector is catching all the fire today: Korea's power utility confirmed a breach touching 1.5 million people, attackers have been slipping out of Azure with stolen API keys tied to energy-sector intrusions, and OT gear is advertising its own insecurity to anyone with a scanner. Meanwhile AI keeps cutting both ways - agents writing eBPF shields to buy patching time on one flank, a $44 billion mega-merger to keep the agent swarm on a leash on the other. Pour something strong.

### Azure API Management Flaw Used to Steal Customer API Keys

Microsoft confirmed a flaw in Azure API Management's Classic tiers was exploited to swipe stored API credentials, with Norwegian authorities tying the attacks to intrusions at energy companies.
Keys parked in a cloud management plane are quiet gold: one config-level bug and every integration secret stored there walks out the front door.

**What to do:** Rotate every API credential stored in APIM, get off the Classic tier, and hunt for odd reuse of your keys across all integrations.

---

### Korean Power Giant KEPCO Confirms Breach Affecting 1.5 Million

Attackers breached the internal network of Korea Electric Power Corporation and reached two systems holding customer, employee, and contractor data on 1.5 million people.
When the national grid operator leaks personal data, the follow-up campaign writes itself - expect KEPCO-flavored phishing aimed at the people who keep the lights on.

**What to do:** Warn staff about utility-themed lures and watch for KEPCO-related data showing up in credential stuffing and phishing lists.

---

### CrowdStrike Buys Zscaler for $44 Billion to Herd AI Agents

CrowdStrike is buying Zscaler for $44 billion, merging endpoint detection with zero-trust access to get a leash on the coming swarm of AI agents.
The largest security acquisition in years is a bet that tomorrow's perimeter is whatever your bots are allowed to touch - and that you want one vendor holding the leash.

**What to do:** If you run either platform, pin down roadmap and licensing impact with your account team now, before "integration synergy" starts showing up on invoices.

---

### OpenAI and Cisco Build AI Agents That Write Exploit Shields for Linux

OpenAI and Cisco open-sourced Shirudo, a Linux runtime where AI agents write eBPF controls on the fly to block exploit behavior while you are still testing the real patch.
It is virtual patching with a model in the loop - clever for buying time, but an agent with eBPF powers in your kernel deserves exactly as much scrutiny as any other root-level tool.

**What to do:** Lab-test it before production, review what its agents can load into the kernel, and never let "AI shields" justify a slow patch cycle.

---

### Industrial Serial Gateways Broadcast Their Own Insecurity

Researchers found critical OT serial gateways announce to the network whether they require authentication - and a depressing number of them announce that they do not.
Your serial-to-Ethernet bridge is telling every scanner on the segment that the drawbridge is down, which is a bold design choice for gear guarding physical processes.

**What to do:** Inventory serial gateways reachable from anything you do not fully control, enable authentication where it exists, and isolate what cannot be fixed.

---

That's the roundup. Stay vigilant, stay patched.
