_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

The plumbing is having a rough week: attackers ran shell commands on the Hex.pm package registry through a decade-old parsing bug, a China-linked crew rooted Palo Alto firewalls, and an AI-driven campaign harvested 24 Check Point management servers across 22 organizations. Even the CIA is on the board, with an unclassified analysis system reportedly breached using an AI-powered pentest framework. Your security stack is the new perimeter - and right now it is the target.

### Ten-Year-Old Parser Bug Hands Attacker RCE on Hex.pm Registry Servers

A CVSS 10.0 insecure deserialization bug (CVE-2026-129514) sitting in the Hex.pm request parser since February 2016 let an unauthenticated attacker run OS commands on six of the Elixir and Erlang registry's production servers on Monday.
No packages were tampered with, but the attacker could have read emails, password hashes, 2FA secrets and private packages - one bad parse away from poisoning the dependency well for half the BEAM world.

**What to do:** If you have a hex.pm account, reset your password and 2FA now, fetch packages only from repo.hex.pm, and update the Hex client when the new signing release lands.

---

### CIA Intelligence Analysis System Breached in Cyberattack

Unknown attackers broke into an unclassified open-source intelligence analysis system at the CIA, with the blast radius reportedly reaching the ODNI and the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency.
Officials say no classified networks were touched, but the intrusion allegedly ran on ARTEX, an AI-powered penetration testing framework - offensive tooling now shipping with an autopilot button.

**What to do:** Treat OSINT and other "unclassified but sensitive" analysis systems as prime targets: review their exposure, tighten access paths, and watch for the signature of automated recon.

---

### AI-Driven Campaign Hijacks 24 Check Point Management Servers

An unattributed crew tracked as GT13 used agentic, AI-driven tooling to compromise 24 Check Point Security Management servers across 22 organizations in 17 countries, then exfiltrated through victim devices and VPN egress.
They stole firewall databases and internal CA files and unloaded security policies - hijack the management server and every firewall it governs politely disarms itself.

**What to do:** Patch CVE-2026-16232, restrict management access to the management network, and hunt for GT13 tradecraft: bulk management tasks, freshly minted certificates, and sudden policy unloads.

---

### Palo Alto Patches Fragnesia Flaw Used to Root Firewalls

A China-linked group chained the PHP2Shell bug with Fragnesia (CVE-2026-46300), a Linux kernel privilege escalation, to plant root-level webshells on 25 servers - almost all PAN-OS firewalls with management interfaces exposed to the internet.
A firewall with a root shell is the worst kind of compromise: full traffic visibility, persistent access, and your own security policies become the attacker's to read.

**What to do:** Install the new PAN-OS builds fixing CVE-2026-46300 and CVE-2026-109618, and get management interfaces off the internet - management network or VPN only.

---

### November Microsoft Patches Break NTLM Authentication

Microsoft's November updates started enforcing NTLM message signing to kill relay attacks and close CVE-2026-118737, a pre-auth RCE in the Windows RPC runtime - and promptly broke logins for third-party SSO, proxy and packaging appliances with loose NTLM implementations.
Hardening that takes your authentication hostage is still hardening: the fix is right, but the fallout lands on every appliance that ever hand-waved the protocol spec.

**What to do:** Hunt for devices failing NTLM auth, use the ClientAllowUnsignedNtlm registry switch only as a stopgap, and push vendors for compliant updates or move those paths to Kerberos.
