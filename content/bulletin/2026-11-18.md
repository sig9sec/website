_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

Today runs on two tracks: unpatched things getting owned and trusted things getting poisoned. A Java flaw hands out pre-auth RCE across your favorite enterprise apps, someone slipped a credential stealer into Microsoft's AI agent toolkit on PyPI, and Russian spies are chaining one cloud login into a third-party mess. Same old playbook, shinier tooling - and as ever, the patch notes are longer than the exploit is hard.

### Java4Shell Hands Out Pre-Auth RCE Across Jira, Jenkins, and Keycloak

A freshly disclosed Java flaw, dubbed Java4Shell and tracked as CVE-2026-114938 (CVSS 8.1), lets attackers run code on Jira, Jenkins, Keycloak, and other widely used apps without an account - the vulnerable code ships in Java 17.0.6 and later.
CISA wants it patched on the double; researchers say reliable exploitation still takes per-target tuning, but pre-auth RCE in your issue tracker and CI server is not a gamble worth taking.

**What to do:** Patch your Java runtimes past the vulnerable builds and treat any internet-facing Jira, Jenkins, or Keycloak as drop-everything urgent.

---

### Someone Poisoned Microsoft's AI Agent Package On PyPI

Attackers pushed trojanized versions of Microsoft's agent-framework package to PyPI (1.26.1 and 1.26.2, published minutes apart), packing a stealer that goes after cloud credentials.
It is the AI-agent gold rush slamming into the same old supply-chain wall: the code you did not write is now holding the keys you did.

**What to do:** Pin and hash your dependencies, scan at install time, and rotate any cloud credentials that touched a freshly updated environment.

---

### Puppet RCE Is Being Exploited In The Wild

A critical RCE in Perforce Puppet - the config-management workhorse behind plenty of enterprise fleets - is under active exploitation, enough that CISA added it to its KEV catalog.
When the tool that pushes your configs is the thing getting owned, the blast radius is roughly everything it manages, which is a polite way to say your whole estate.

**What to do:** Patch Puppet against the KEV deadline, audit your compile and master servers, and alert on unexpected config changes or new node registrations.

---

### COLD RELIC Turns One Cloud Login Into A Third-Party Problem

Russia-linked COLD RELIC (the group you knew as COLDRIVER) weaponized a single Google Workspace compromise into linked intrusions, using stolen cloud data to breach a second U.S. organization.
Mandiant's writeup is a tidy case study in how one tenant breach rarely stays politely inside one tenant.

**What to do:** Treat cloud tokens and OAuth grants as high-value loot - enforce MFA with device binding, scope service accounts tightly, and alert on unusual Workspace data access.

---

### A Scattered Spider Ringleader Draws Six Years

A leader of the Scattered Spider collective was sentenced to six years in a U.S. prison for stealing millions in cryptocurrency and conspiring to hack a dozen companies.
It will not dismantle the crew - their social-engineering playbook is effectively open source - but it is satisfying to see a SIM-swapping ring catch an actual consequence.

**What to do:** Harden the helpdesk against social engineering: verify callers out-of-band and retire SMS-based account resets.
