_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

Patch Tuesday came in like an avalanche: Microsoft alone shipped fixes for 1,259 flaws, including an Outlook zero-day already being exploited in the wild. AI security had its own moment, with research showing you can backdoor an agent by tweaking a handful of neurons and stolen API keys running up a $740,000 tab. Pace yourself - this triage will take a while.

### Patch Tuesday Drops 1,259 Fixes And An Exploited Outlook Zero-Day

Microsoft patched 1,259 vulnerabilities this month, more than 700 of them in Windows, and the headline act is an Outlook flaw under active attack that can expose NTLMv2 authentication hashes.
It is the second month in a row with a four-digit CVE count, and those hashes are a shortcut to credential relay attacks across your Windows estate.

**What to do:** Patch the exploited Outlook bug first, then anything that puts Outlook or Exchange on the network, and triage the rest by exposure rather than CVSS score.

---

### Backdooring An AI Agent Takes Fewer Than Ten Neurons

Microsoft researchers demonstrated that editing fewer than 10 rarely-used neurons in a model - no retraining required - plants a hidden backdoor that makes it emit attacker commands when it sees a trigger phrase.
The agent running that model then executes those commands with its own permissions, so model weights are officially part of your supply chain now.

**What to do:** Verify model provenance and pin hashes before deploying models into agents, and give agents the least privilege you can live with so a poisoned model stays a nuisance instead of a disaster.

---

### Stolen AI Keys Rack Up $740,000 In Unauthorized Compute

Platform provider Railway confirmed that intruders who hit it in October made off with AI provider keys stored by customers and burned through an estimated $740,000 in unauthorized usage charges.
Railway is eating the bill wherever the AI providers will not refund it, which is generous - but the real lesson is that secrets parked in platform dashboards become someone else's shopping spree.

**What to do:** Keep AI provider keys in a secrets manager with spend limits and usage alerts, and rotate anything that ever lived in a platform dashboard.

---

### Telecom Messaging Flaws Exploited In The Wild Despite Patches

CISA added two telecom bugs (CVE-2026-111793 and CVE-2026-20320) in Openwave's Webtop Media Transcoder and Cisco BroadWorks to its actively exploited catalog, both allowing leaks of configuration data and other sensitive information.
Carrier-grade messaging platforms hold call routing, voicemail, and identity data, and the patches were already available - the attackers simply got there first.

**What to do:** If you run BroadWorks or Openwave, patch now and look for signs of data exposure; otherwise ask your telco provider which of these they run on your behalf.

---

### Apple Patches Pairing Flaws And A Brotli Bug Under Attack

Apple's latest security updates fix Bluetooth and AirPlay pairing vulnerabilities, iMessage flaws, and a Brotli decompression bug that is already being exploited in real attacks.
Pairing and parsing flaws need nothing more than a nearby device or a crafted file, and Apple gear patches on its own sweet time if you let it.

**What to do:** Push the updates across macOS, iOS, iPadOS, and everything else Apple in your environment today, and verify that managed devices actually installed them.
