_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

The FBI confiscated the toolkit of a Chinese state-linked crew, Citrix dropped another drop-everything patch for the edge, and AI agents spent the day on both sides of the fence: hijacking cloud fleets and filing bug reports with the maintainers. Somewhere in there, a chatbot probably asked itself whether going rogue was on the calendar.

### FBI Seizes Flax Typhoon Toolkit In Critical Infrastructure Sweep

The FBI and DOJ seized seven domains used by China-linked Flax Typhoon to run its MicroScan and FishHub platforms, disrupting tooling built to scan and compromise critical infrastructure worldwide. Domain seizures don't evict intruders who are already inside, so treat this as a headwind for the attackers, not a cleanup of your network.

**What to do:** Hunt your logs for the seized infrastructure and Flax Typhoon IOCs, and assume anyone pre-positioned in OT segments is still there until proven otherwise.

---

### Critical NetScaler RCE Demands Immediate Patching

Citrix is pushing admins to patch CVE-2026-107406 right now: a critical flaw in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway that can lead to remote code execution or denial-of-service. The track record of NetScaler bugs becoming mass-exploitation fodder within days is long and embarrassing, and ransomware crews read advisories too.

**What to do:** Inventory every NetScaler box, patch immediately, and check for indicators of compromise before declaring victory - a patched but owned appliance is still owned.

---

### One Prompt, Whole Fleet: AgentCorruption Exposed AWS Agents To Hijacking

A now-patched vulnerability in AWS Bedrock AgentCore, dubbed AgentCorruption, let an attacker corrupt a single AI agent and leverage it to compromise an organization's entire agent fleet. When every agent shares credentials and trust, one poisoned prompt is effectively a domain admin with better manners.

**What to do:** Update AgentCore, give each agent its own least-privilege IAM role, and design for the assumption that any single agent will eventually go rogue.

---

### Anthropic Starts Automating Bug Reports For Open Source Maintainers

Anthropic's new OSS Scanner fast-tracks unreviewed, model-generated vulnerability reports straight to opted-in maintainers, alongside a push tapping 11 firms to secure OT environments. AI filing tickets at machine speed sounds like progress until you remember maintainers are humans with finite hours - expect the signal-to-noise debate to get spicy.

**What to do:** If you maintain OSS, set an explicit policy for AI-generated reports, and if you consume them, verify before you patch - plausible-looking findings still need reproduction.
