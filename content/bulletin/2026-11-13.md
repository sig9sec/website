_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

Fresh Windows zero-day in the wild, attackers turning Check Point gateways into ESXi-wiping disasters, and the FBI casually recovering $1.2 billion in stolen crypto. Add a stack of critical Citrix patches and 354 dark web vendors learning that "anonymous" is a marketing term, and you've got a day that rewards the paranoid. Here's what matters and what to do about it.

### Nightmare Eclipse Drops SignDrop Windows Zero-Day In The Wild

The Nightmare Eclipse group is now wielding SignDrop, a Windows zero-day exploit that chains a flaw in the Common Log File System (CLFS) with Windows Defender to create arbitrary files running as the antimalware process.
When your security tool becomes the dropper, most defenses politely look the other way - code executing inside Defender's context is about as sneaky as a payload can get.

**What to do:** Treat abnormal file creation under Defender directories as a red alert, monitor for CLFS-related crashes, and apply Microsoft's fix the moment it lands.

---

### Check Point Gateways Hit By PHP2Shell And A TSGA Backdoor That Wipes ESXi

Attackers are breaking into unpatched Check Point Security Gateways through the PHP2Shell flaws, then abusing an undisclosed Gaia OS privilege-escalation bug (CVE-2026-123039) to reach root and install a backdoor called TSGA.
They don't stop at spying - they wipe files on attached ESXi hosts, turning a firewall breach into a virtualization catastrophe with a very short fuse.

**What to do:** Patch the PHP2Shell flaws and the Gaia OS LPE immediately, hunt for TSGA artifacts on your gateways, and keep ESXi backups that a compromised gateway can't touch.

---

### FBI Seizes $1.2 Billion In Cryptocurrency Stolen From Binance

The FBI says US authorities recovered $1.2 billion worth of cryptocurrency taken from Binance, and separately pinned a $400 million crypto heist on North Korea's social engineering crews.
It's a rare good day for asset recovery - and a reminder that nation-state hackers still drain exchanges with nothing fancier than a convincing conversation.

**What to do:** Keep significant holdings off exchanges where you can, and drill finance and ops teams on social engineering - the most expensive attacks still start with a phone call or a DM.

---

### Citrix Patches 10 Critical NetScaler Vulnerabilities

Citrix dropped fixes for 10 critical flaws in NetScaler ADC and Gateway that can cause denial-of-service or other unexpected behavior when exploited.
NetScaler lives at the edge of a huge number of corporate networks, so this is a "patch it today" bulletin, not a "add it to next sprint" one.

**What to do:** Apply the Citrix updates now, lock management interfaces to trusted networks only, and scan your instances for signs of probing or config tampering.

---

### Global Crackdown Nets 354 Suspects Across Five Dark Web Markets

Law enforcement swept up 354 vendors and buyers in a joint operation hitting the Archetyp, Nemesis, Tor2Door, Bohemia, and Kingdom dark web marketplaces.
"Anonymous" marketplaces keep turning out to be thoroughly infiltrated, which is reassuring or terrifying depending on which side of the trade you're on.

**What to do:** Nothing to patch here, but if your data surfaces on these markets, assume law enforcement, competitors, and every reseller already have a copy - and plan your response accordingly.
