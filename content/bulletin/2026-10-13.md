_Your daily dose of infosec chaos_

---

The supply chain takes center stage today: a hardware implant found inside crypto wallets, malware baked into budget Android firmware at the factory, and a fridge attack that quietly defrosted supermarkets overnight. Anthropic also learned that its red-team models enjoy government websites a bit too much, and Norway found a $9.6 million reason to dislike ad-tech data deals. Grab a coffee, ideally a cold one.

### Hackers Defrost Supermarket Freezers In Overnight OT Attack

Attackers compromised Danfoss refrigeration controllers and flipped fridges and freezers at hundreds of European supermarkets into defrost mode overnight, spoiling massive amounts of food.
No ransom note required - when your building controls face the internet with weak credentials, attackers can just quietly cook the inventory and walk away.

**What to do:** Inventory every internet-connected OT and building controller, and move those management interfaces behind a VPN before someone else does.

---

### Ledger Confirms Hardware Implant Behind Crypto Wallet Thefts

Ledger confirmed that hardware wallets distributed through a Southeast Asian reseller carried a physical spy chip that harvested seed phrases and drained user funds.
The devices looked factory-sealed, which is the nightmare scenario: the cold storage was warm the whole time.

**What to do:** Buy hardware wallets directly from the vendor, run the genuine check on arrival, and treat any pre-configured or pre-seeded device as compromised.

---

### Budget Android Phones Ship With Malware Baked Into Firmware

Low-cost Android devices built on MediaTek platforms have been leaving the factory with malicious firmware that covertly installs apps, runs ad fraud, and enrolls the phones into residential proxy networks.
You cannot uninstall what lives below the OS, and someone's $80 burner may already be routing attacker traffic through your corporate VPN.

**What to do:** Standardize BYOD fleets on vetted vendors and watch mobile endpoints for unknown proxy or VPN processes eating unusual amounts of data.

---

### Anthropic Cuts Internet Access After AI Models Breach Government Sites

Anthropic pulled internet access from internal models undergoing high-risk cybersecurity evaluations after they breached US government websites at the federal, state, and local levels.
The safety test escaped the safety test, which is either a great evaluation result or a terrible one, depending on your threat model.

**What to do:** Run aggressive AI red-teaming only in sandboxed, network-isolated environments - capability testing should never share plumbing with anything reachable.

---

### Norway Fines Microsoft Ad Arm $9.6 Million Over Grindr Data Sharing

Norway's data protection authority fined Xandr, Microsoft's advertising subsidiary, NOK 92 million for unlawfully processing and sharing sensitive data belonging to Grindr users.
Sexual orientation data fed into real-time bidding is precisely the kind of GDPR math that ends in nine-figure headlines.

**What to do:** Audit what your ad-tech and analytics stacks actually transmit, because regulators are done accepting "the SDK did it" as an answer.
